INTEGRATION TRACK integrate_team_spec - the team's architecture, built and measured
Design data only (harness.load('design'), rows 744+, 282 days, default costs unless "stress"). About 75 backtested
variants (15 of them placebo labelings, 5 benchmarks/references) plus 17 fresh-start window runs.

VERDICT
The architecture as specified (K-Means regime -> EMA crossover in "trend", z-score mean reversion in "chop") does not
work, and the reason is structural, not a matter of tuning:
  - An EMA crossover happens when the trend is weakest. At the hour EMA48 crosses EMA200 the K-Means label says
    "trend" for only 19% of up-crosses and 8% of down-crosses. The switch therefore blocks 80-90% of the leg's entries.
  - A 168 h z-score of -3 is a strong directional fall. At those hours the label says "chop" only 14% of the time.
    The switch blocks 86% of the mean-reversion entries and keeps the worse ones.
  - The label carries no information for this map: swapping the map (EMA in "chop", mean reversion in "trend") earned
    +115%, the specified map -5.5%, and giving each coin another coin's label path did as well as the real labels.
The two legs themselves, run side by side with separate budgets and no switch, are the best form of the team's idea:
+85% on design with an 11% drawdown (hourly-close stops), better ratios than the tuned breakout, positive in 69% of
fresh-start 11-day windows. The BTC "long when good, short when bad" gate also subtracts (+95% -> +37% on the EMA leg);
the crossover already goes long on up-crosses and short on down-crosses per coin, which is the same intent done better.

LEG SETTINGS (taken from the first round, not re-tuned)
  EMA leg: EMA 48/200, entry within 3 bars of the cross, 6 x ATR(24) trailing stop, exit on the opposite cross, at most
           4 positions, size = min(24.75% of equity, 1% of equity / stop distance). Reproduces ema_risk/candidate_1
           order for order (+94.7% under the current harness; the +101% quoted earlier was before the stop-overshoot change).
  MR leg : 168 h z <= -3.0, long only, market entry, sell at first close with z >= 0 or after 48 h, 20% disaster stop,
           at most 10 positions, 12 h cooldown. Market-only (no scale-in limit, no resting sale): +15.3% at 10 x 9.9%,
           the same as z_risk/candidate_2 (+15.2%), so the resting orders are not needed.
NEW CHOICES MADE HERE (each stated, few)
  1. Capital split: EMA leg keeps its risk sizing (about 12.5% of equity a position, 4 positions); MR positions are 5%
     of equity each, at most 10. Tried 2.5 / 5 / 7.5 / 9.9%: +99 / +110 / +102 / +110% (intrabar stops). Flat: not a tuned value.
  2. K-Means: K=2, the 'core4' trend/chop features of the regime_trendchop track, first fit on 456 h so labels exist
     from row 723, refit every 336 h on all past data. Agrees 99.8% with regime.walk_forward(use_hmm=False).
  3. A only: entry allowed up to 12 bars after the cross (3 / 12 / 24 tried: -5.5 / +23.4 / +20.9%). 12 is the best of
     three, so expect less. Needed because the label lags the cross.
  4. B only: stops judged on the hourly close instead of inside the hour (less live code). One continuous run: +85%
     against +110%; fresh 11-day windows: mean +2.79% / median +2.24% against +2.68% / +1.88%. No real difference.

WHAT EACH PART ADDS OR SUBTRACTS
  K-Means regime switch : subtracts. EMA+MR without it +110% (intrabar stops); with it -5.5% (strict) or +23% (12-bar
                          entry window). No better than shuffled or delayed labels. A plain threshold (|EMA24-EMA100|/ATR
                          > 1.25) does a little better than K-Means (+41% against +23%) and is still far below no switch.
                          One real but small effect, already known: EMA LONGS taken 6-24 h after the cross in the "trend"
                          state (A's long side +33.5k; delayed labels: +14% against +45% for the EMA leg, rows 1750+).
  Mean-reversion leg    : in the "chop" state it loses (-6.6k alone, -10.5k inside A). Unswitched and small it adds +5 points
                          at default costs and nothing at stress costs (+58.7% with, +59.1% without). What it does give:
                          drawdown 13.2% -> 11.0%, worst fresh window -11.5% -> -8.5%, days with a fill 70% -> 82%, and a
                          daily-return correlation of -0.35 with the EMA leg. It is a stabiliser, not a profit source.
                          All of its profit came in bad markets; restricted to good markets it lost 6.6k.
  BTC direction rule    : subtracts 58 points from the EMA leg (+95% -> +37%); the reversed rule made +25%, so the rule
                          just throws away half the trades. Do not add it to this design. (It did help the breakout
                          longs in round one; that is a different strategy.)
  Shorts                : half of B's profit (+40.7k of +83.5k). Long-only B is +32% with a 25% drawdown and a negative
                          median fresh window: without shorts B is not worth deploying.

CAVEATS
  1. B is the first-round EMA leg plus a small stabiliser. It adds no new evidence for that leg; it inherits its risks.
  2. The EMA leg is sensitive to the entry window: entry within 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 / 12 / 24 bars of the cross gives
     +89 / +93 / +95 / +77 / +50 / +40 / +17%. The live bot must act in the first three hourly bars after a cross; a bot
     that is down for a few hours misses the trade and must not chase it.
  3. Long-only made +25% and short-only +30% but both together +95%: about a third of the result comes from which
     signals win one of the four slots, not from the signals. Treat +85% as an upper estimate.
  4. The design period was a bear market (average coin -45%). The short side may be riding it. Two 30-day blocks carry
     most of B's profit and the last third is -5% (close stops) / +5% (intrabar stops).
  5. Against the tuned breakout, B has a lower mean (fresh windows +2.8% against +5.4%) and far fewer +10% windows
     (12% against 33%), but a higher median (+2.2% against +1.6%), a higher win rate (69% against 53%), a third of the
     <= -5% windows (8% against 24%) and better Sortino/Sharpe/Calmar. Which matters depends on whether the team needs
     to climb into the top 20 on return (breakout) or protect ratios (B).
  6. Hourly-close stops leave a short exposed to a one-hour squeeze. A position is at most 24.75% of equity, so a +30%
     hour would cost up to 7.4% of equity. This did not happen often enough on design to show, but it can.
  7. Nothing here was run on the selection or holdout data.
