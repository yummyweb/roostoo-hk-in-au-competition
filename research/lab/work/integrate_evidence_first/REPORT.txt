ANSWER IN ONE PARAGRAPH
Run the two parts that survived side by side, each in its own capital sleeve: tuned breakout longs behind the BTC>EMA480
gate, and the EMA 48/200 long+short crossover with a 6 x ATR trail sized by risk. On design the mix made +175% with a 16.5%
drawdown (Sharpe 2.79, Sortino 6.73, Calmar 16.4), against +162% / 27.5% (2.13 / 5.13 / 9.00) for the breakout alone and
+79% / 12.9% (2.36 / 4.71 / 8.72) for the EMA leg alone: better than either leg on all three ratios. It needs no intrabar
stops and no limit orders, but it does need short open/close. The best long-only version is the gated breakout alone;
nothing long-only that was added to it improved it. The K-Means regime filter, the z-score leg, the BTC gate on the EMA leg,
shared slots and a portfolio drawdown brake are all left out because each one failed to add anything.

DECISIONS (each is one choice; alternatives tried are listed)
1. Separate sleeves, not shared slots. Shared slots gave +115% / 22.5% (4), +156% / 18.2% (6), +140% / 22.2% (8): not
   monotonic, and EMA positions crowd out breakout entries (44-93 breakout trades instead of about 139).
2. Split 1:1 between breakout and EMA. Sleeves at 100% total (EMA risk per trade = 1% of its sleeve):
     100/0  +162.1%  27.5%  2.13 / 5.13 /  9.00   thirds +76 / +33 / +12
      70/30 +136.9%  16.3%  2.48 / 6.19 / 12.60          +51 / +40 / +12
      50/50 +121.5%  12.9%  2.75 / 6.59 / 13.94          +44 / +46 /  +6
      30/70 +104.7%  12.7%  2.88 / 6.35 / 12.05          +35 / +51 /  +0
       0/100 +79.4%  12.9%  2.36 / 4.71 /  8.72          +24 / +57 /  -8
   Every mix from 30/70 to 70/30 beats both legs alone on drawdown-adjusted terms and on all three ratios; 50/50 is the
   centre. This is a plateau, not a peak.
3. Scale: 67% + 67%. The EMA leg sized by risk rarely uses more than half of its sleeve, so a 50/50 split leaves a third of
   the account idle. Same 1:1 composition at larger scale: 50% +121.5% / 12.9% (2.75 / 6.59 / 13.9); 60% +142.6% / 15.2%
   (2.67 / 6.32 / 14.1); 67% +175.0% / 16.5% (2.79 / 6.73 / 16.4); 75% +194.6% / 18.6% (2.74 / 6.70 / 16.4). Sharpe and
   Sortino do not move, return and drawdown scale together: this is a risk dial, not an edge. 67% is where the account is
   about fully used without the legs fighting for cash. If the team prefers a smaller drawdown, set both to 50%.
   Not chosen: 100% + 100% (cash-limited) printed +269% / 19% (Calmar 23), but its neighbours (100/80, 90/100, 100/120,
   85/85) gave +174% to +209% with Calmar 14-17. It is a lucky peak created by which breakout entries were crowded out.
4. BTC gate stays on the breakout leg only. Steering the EMA leg with it (longs only in good markets, shorts only in bad)
   cut the 50/50 mix from +121.5% to +81.5% and every ratio (2.10 / 4.83 / 7.13), and the EMA leg alone from +79% to +27%.
   Removing the gate from the breakout inside the mix is also worse: +170% but 26% drawdown (2.30 / 5.55 / 10.0).
5. EMA stop judged on the hourly close, not intrabar. Leg alone: close +79.4% / 12.9% (2.36 / 4.71 / 8.72) vs intrabar
   +94.8% / 15.3% (2.52 / 5.28 / 8.92). Inside the chosen mix the difference disappears: close +175.0% / 16.5%
   (2.79 / 6.73 / 16.4, fresh 11-day mean +4.53%) vs intrabar +183.4% / 17.7% (2.83 / 6.81 / 16.1, fresh mean +4.41%).
   The hourly-close version is preferred: same result, no one-minute stop code. Its weak spot is the last third of the EMA
   leg (-8% alone, vs +2% intrabar).
6. Slots: 4 per leg, as in the parts. 6 breakout slots: 2.76 / 5.79 / 12.2; 6 EMA slots: 2.47 / 4.92 / 9.8; at most 2
   shorts: 2.55 / 5.64 / 11.8. All a little worse; no reason to change.
7. No portfolio brake. "No new entries while equity is X below its 7-day high": on the mix X = 8%, 10%, 15% never or almost
   never binds (results unchanged) and X = 5% costs a third of the return (+121.5% -> +83.7%, ratios 2.20 / 4.97 / 10.3).
   On the long-only breakout: 5% +105%, 8% +181%, 10% +155%, 15% +162% (no effect). The 8% reading is one spike between two
   worse neighbours: noise. The rule does not help.
8. No z-score sleeve. Added to the 50/50 mix from idle cash: 10% -> 2.76 / 6.55 / 13.7, 20% -> 2.80 / 6.57 / 14.7 (base
   2.75 / 6.59 / 13.9); it earned $2-5k on about 270 trades and needs resting limits and a disaster stop. With hourly
   market orders only it earned +7% alone and -0.2% after stress. It neither helps nor hurts the ratios in any way that is
   distinguishable from noise. Its only use is activity (it raises the long-only candidate from 40% to about 71% of days
   with a fill at about zero expected profit).
9. No K-Means / HMM regime switch. Round one showed the labels carry no information about which family pays; the only
   "regime" input that earns its place is the one-line BTC>EMA480 gate on new breakout longs.

  (c) position size from the stop distance: usd = min(16.6% of equity, 0.67% of equity x close / (6 x ATR24));
  (d) a per-position trailing level and leg label kept across restarts; (e) one net position per coin (a coin held by one
  leg is skipped by the other). No intrabar stops, no limit orders, no minute-level decisions.

CAVEATS
- Design data only, and a falling market. The short side's profit is partly the bear market (round one measured its beta
  at about -0.4 to -0.5 and it lost in every rising stretch). In a rally the EMA shorts will cost money; the breakout
  longs are expected to offset that, but no rising period long enough to prove it is in this data.
- The last third is the weak one: candidate 1 made +2.1% (-1.9% after stress) and its EMA leg lost $18k there. Round-one
  verification already said to expect about half of the EMA leg's headline. Treat +175% as an upper bound of what was seen.
- Profits come from a handful of large moves (10 best trades = all of the net profit). Over 11 days the result depends on
  whether one or two such moves occur: the median fresh window is +2.6% for candidate 1 and 0.0% for candidate 2.
- Candidate 2 does nothing while BTC is under its 480 h EMA. If the competition needs trades on 8 of 14 days this is a
  real risk; the z sleeve or a tiny EMA-long sleeve buys activity at roughly zero (z) or negative (EMA long) expected value.
- Stops act on the hourly close: a fast crash inside an hour is not capped. Shorts have no intrabar stop either.
- The 67% scale, the 1:1 split and the hourly-close stop were chosen on design. They sit on plateaus, but the selection and
  holdout periods have not been looked at by this track.
