WHAT THE TEAM ASKED FOR, AND WHAT IS IN THE TWO DESIGNS
  Asked: K-Means regime filter -> EMA crossover or z-score mean reversion; short bad markets, long good ones; maker orders.
  Delivered: breakout longs + EMA 48/200 crossover long AND short, every position sized by risk at its stop, market orders.
  Left out because the first round showed they lose, and nothing here changed that:
    - z-score mean reversion (no edge after costs in 675 window settings);
    - the K-Means / HMM regime label (no information about which family pays);
    - maker-only execution (the limit orders that miss are the winners).
  The only regime filter that earned its place is a plain one: BTC hourly close above its 480 h EMA, used ONLY to allow
  new breakout longs (balanced design). Using the same label to switch the EMA leg (longs in good markets, shorts in bad)
  made the EMA leg worse: fresh 11-day mean +1.5% against +2.5% ungated, and Sortino 2.3 against 6.4.

HOW IT WAS JUDGED
  Fresh start: the strategy is restarted flat with $100k at the start of every 11-day window (91 windows at a 3-day step,
  136 at a 2-day step), indicators already warm. Only about 26 of these windows are independent, and only 38 of 91 had a
  rising market (the design period was a bear market). 90% intervals below come from a block bootstrap over windows.

INTEGRATION CHOICES MADE HERE (everything else is a first-round plateau centre)
  1. Which legs: breakout longs + EMA 48/200 long+short. Daily-return correlation between the two legs 0.28 to 0.34
     (0.25 to 0.42 on 11-day window returns), so they diversify, and the EMA leg fixes the activity problem.
  2. Gate: BTC > EMA(480 h) on new breakout longs in the balanced design only. In a mix it raises the share of windows
     above zero (71% against 66%) and cuts the worst window (-10.8% against -14.4%) for 0.3 points of mean. Alone it leaves
     the account idle (no trade at all in 24% of windows), so it must not be run without the EMA leg.
  3. Sizing: risk at the stop instead of equal slots. Breakout at 2% risk has the same mean as equal slots (+5.45%) with a
     worst window of -9.8% instead of -15.9%. Risk grid 0.5 / 1 / 2% only. Balanced: each leg at the largest grid value at
     which that leg alone keeps its worst window inside -8% (breakout 1%: -5.3%; EMA 0.5%: -5.9%; EMA 1% fails at -10.8%).
     Aggressive: both doubled.
  4. EMA stop judged on the hourly close instead of intrabar: same result in the mix on fresh windows (mean +4.78% against
     +4.85%, worst -12.6% against -14.4%) and it needs no minute-level stop code. In the 9-month continuous run the
     close-based version of the aggressive design made less (+173% against +268%), so this is not free everywhere.
  5. Shared cash, exits first, breakout entries before EMA entries, entry skipped if free cash is below half its size.

WHAT SUBTRACTS VALUE OR DOES NOT HELP (said plainly)
  - Z-score mean reversion and the K-Means regime label: no edge; not included.
  - "Short when the market is bad": timing shorts with the BTC label is worse than not timing them. EMA shorts alone are a
    bet on a falling market (rising windows: 26% above zero). The symmetric long+short EMA leg is what survives.
  - A standing BTC short: +1.5 points per window when the market fell, -2.3 points when it rose. A direction bet, not an edge.
  - The BTC gate without an always-active leg: median window 0%, 4.6 active days of 11, fails the 8-of-14-days rule.
  - 6 breakout slots instead of 4: lower mean, same share of winning windows.
  - Equal slots: larger worst window for the same mean. EMA at 2% risk or equal slots: worst window -19% to -21%.
  - In the aggressive design the EMA leg takes cash from the breakout leg: breakout alone at 2% risk made +213% in the
    continuous run and +5.45% per fresh window; with the EMA leg the totals are +173% and +5.80%. The EMA leg there buys
    a better median (+3.5% against +1.5%) and activity (9.9 days against 7.4), not a higher return.

LIVE REQUIREMENTS
  Both designs decide once per hour from completed hourly candles and send market orders only. No resting limits and no
  intrabar stops. The one new capability is opening and covering 1x shorts at market for the EMA leg. If shorts cannot be
  made to work, drop the whole EMA leg (EMA long-only has no edge): the fallback is breakout longs risk-sized at 1%
  (mean +3.6%, median +1.0%, above zero 58%, worst -5.3%) or 2% (mean +5.45%, median +1.5%, above zero 57%, worst -9.8%), both
  ungated, with fills on 7.4 days of 11 and 7 or more active days in only 64% of windows.
  Data: 600 hourly candles per coin (3 x 200 for the slow EMA; 480 for the BTC gate), 50 pairs.

CAVEATS
  - About 26 independent 11-day windows; every probability above carries roughly +/- 14 points. The step of the windows
    alone moves the balanced design's share above zero from 75% to 68%.
  - The design period is one bear market. Both legs were weakest in its last third (balanced +1.6% per window, aggressive -0.3%).
  - First-round fragility carries over: five trades supply the breakout profit; the EMA centre is a local peak (expect about half).
  - Both legs were selected on this same data; the combination adds two sizing numbers, one gate switch and one stop style.
  - Short fills, short fees and the cap on short losses are modelled, not measured on the real exchange.
